# Why long-term borrowing costs may stay elevated despite expected Fed rate cuts

Federal Reserve rate cuts may not significantly lower long-term borrowing costs across the economy. Even if the central bank eases monetary policy, long-term borrowing rates are poised to remain elevated in a yield curve bear steepener. In this environment, short-term policy rates drift downward while long-term yields stay sticky, driven by term premiums and heavy market debt issuance.

While policy easing lowers the front end of the yield curve, market forecasts indicate that the 10-year Treasury yield will remain anchored well above policy rates, sustaining higher capital costs for corporations and maintaining valuation pressures on long-duration assets.

### Mechanics of the bear steepener

A bear steepener occurs when long-term interest rates face upward pressure or remain persistently elevated even as short-term policy rates decline, widening the spread between both ends of the yield curve.

On the short end, consensus expectations point toward monetary easing. According to analysis published by [IMTC](https://imtc.com/insights/fixed-income-outlook-2026), most Fed watchers project two interest rate cuts in 2026, which would lower the federal funds target range to 3.0% to 3.5% by year-end. Under standard mechanics, this expected policy easing acts to anchor or pull down short-duration yields.

At the long end of the curve, however, yields are not projected to drop in tandem. The Breckinridge Investment Committee forecasts that the benchmark 10-year Treasury yield will remain range-bound between 4.0% and 4.5%, as reported by [Breckinridge Capital Advisors](https://www.breckinridge.com/insights/february-2026-market-commentary). This divergence, with policy rates settling at 3.0% to 3.5% while the 10-year yield holds between 4.0% and 4.5%, illustrates the structural steepening dynamic: long-dated debt demands an independent term premium instead of tracking central bank policy downward.

While the Breckinridge Investment Committee expects the benchmark 10-year yield to remain range-bound between 4.0% and 4.5% according to [Breckinridge Capital Advisors](https://www.breckinridge.com/insights/february-2026-market-commentary), other analysts note that a bear steepening could push the yield as high as 4.50% or even higher, as reported by [IMTC](https://imtc.com/insights/fixed-income-outlook-2026).

The fixed-income market has also had to absorb heavy supply. According to SIFMA figures cited by [IMTC](https://imtc.com/insights/fixed-income-outlook-2026), U.S. corporations issued roughly $1.7 trillion in investment-grade debt in 2025, approaching the historic peak established in 2020. Furthermore, [IMTC](https://imtc.com/insights/fixed-income-outlook-2026) points out that large and rising U.S. fiscal deficits require increased Treasury issuance, which, together with inflation hovering near 3%, places persistent upward pressure on long-term Treasury yields.

### Implications for corporate refinancing and equity valuations

The divergence between short and long rates carries direct operational and valuation consequences across markets.

While the roughly $1.7 trillion in investment-grade issuance cited by [IMTC](https://imtc.com/insights/fixed-income-outlook-2026) addressed immediate corporate funding and liquidity, it established substantial refinancing commitments. Because policy cuts at the front end do not automatically compress long-duration borrowing costs, corporate issuers face higher debt-servicing expenses during upcoming rollover cycles unless long-end yields drop materially.

Equity valuations face similar constraints from persistent long rates. According to [State Street Global Advisors](https://www.ssga.com/sg/en/individual/library-content/assets/pdf/global/wmu/2026/mom-20260921.pdf), higher yields increase the discount rate applied to future cash flows, compressing valuation multiples. Over the year leading into September 2026, the S&P 500 forward price-to-earnings multiple compressed from roughly 23x to approximately 19x as the 10-year real yield climbed to 2.6% ([State Street Global Advisors](https://www.ssga.com/sg/en/individual/library-content/assets/pdf/global/wmu/2026/mom-20260921.pdf)). Furthermore, as Treasury yields moved toward 5%, the equity risk premium remained near lows, demonstrating that sticky long-term yields continue to impose a tangible valuation headwind even while central banks ease policy ([State Street Global Advisors](https://www.ssga.com/sg/en/individual/library-content/assets/pdf/global/wmu/2026/mom-20260921.pdf)).

For borrowers and investors, central bank rate cuts will not necessarily translate into cheap capital. The persistence of 10-year Treasury yields in the 4.0% to 4.5% range means that long-duration financing will remain relatively expensive. A lower federal funds rate provides relief to cash markets, but structural term premiums embedded in long-dated bonds dictate the true cost of corporate borrowing and equity risk pricing.
