# Stagflation in 2026: How Rising Oil, Sticky Inflation, and Labor Market Cracks Are Repricing Every Asset Class

The convergence of rising oil prices, elevated inflation, and labor market deterioration has created a macroeconomic environment that bears uncomfortable resemblance to the stagflationary episodes of the 1970s. As of late March 2026, the word **stagflation** is no longer a theoretical risk buried in tail-scenario modelsit is an active force repricing equities, fixed income, and credit markets in real time. Nuveen's Fixed Income Weekly Commentary, published March 30, 2026, captured the inflection point in a single observation: Middle East conflict is driving oil prices and Treasury yields higher, while spreads widen across fixed income markets ([Nuveen Fixed Income Insights](https://www.nuveen.com/global/insights/fixed-income)). No single datapoint in isolation would warrant alarm. But the simultaneous pressure across energy costs, inflation persistence, and employment softening poses a distinct challenge: monetary policy tools designed to address inflation work at cross-purposes with those needed to support jobs. It is this paralysis at the heart of the Federal Reserve's dual mandatenot any one indicatorthat functions as the central transmission mechanism repricing risk across asset classes. The margin for policy error has narrowed considerably, and the implications for portfolio construction are immediate.

This article maps the transmission chain from the current geopolitical and macro shock through to market effects, examines the highest-signal evidence available, lays out scenario frameworks for the remainder of 2026, and identifies the concrete triggers investors should monitor in the weeks ahead.

## The Stagflation Transmission Chain: From Oil Shock to Market Repricing

The stagflation transmission mechanism operates not as a single shock but as a simultaneous three-channel assault on corporate margins, consumer purchasing power, and asset valuations. Understanding these channelsand their interactionsis essential to grasping why the current environment is qualitatively different from a standard recession or a garden-variety inflation scare.

### Channel 1: Energy Costs  Margin Compression

When oil prices spike, the first-order effect is a direct increase in input costs for corporates reliant on energy-intensive production and transportation. Airlines, industrials, and consumer discretionary firms face immediate margin pressure as fuel, freight, and raw material costs rise faster than they can reprice their output. The timing lag is critical: commodity contracts, hedging programs, and fixed-price customer agreements mean that cost pass-through is neither instant nor uniform. Firms with thin margins or high energy intensity absorb the blow first, while those with pricing power delay the impactbut do not escape it.

The widening of fixed income spreads noted by Nuveen in late March 2026 suggests that credit markets are already pricing in deteriorating corporate fundamentals for exposed sectors ([Nuveen Fixed Income Insights](https://www.nuveen.com/global/insights/fixed-income)). It is uncertain, however, exactly which sectors are experiencing the widest spread expansion, as granular data is not available in the source material.

### Channel 2: Energy and Food Pass-Through  Real Wage Erosion  Consumer Pullback

The second channel operates with a longer lag but carries more durable macroeconomic consequences. As energy and food prices filter into headline consumer price indices, real wages erodeparticularly for lower- and middle-income households whose consumption baskets are disproportionately weighted toward essentials. This erosion in purchasing power leads to a pullback in discretionary spending, which in turn feeds back into corporate revenues and, eventually, into payrolls.

The negative feedback looprising prices reducing demand, reduced demand triggering layoffs, layoffs further depressing demandis the hallmark of stagflationary dynamics. Historical precedent from the 1970s and early 2020s suggests this loop can take two to four quarters to fully manifest, though the precise timing depends on the persistence of the supply shock and the degree of labor market tightness at the onset.

### Channel 3: Sticky Inflation  Monetary Tightening  Multiple Compression

The third and most mechanically direct channel runs through the discount rate. When inflation proves sticky, central banks face a constrained choice set: hold rates high or hike further, even as growth deteriorates. Higher risk-free rates mechanically increase the discount rate applied to future cash flows, expanding the equity risk premium and compressing price-to-earnings multiplesespecially for long-duration growth stocks whose valuations depend on distant earnings.

The rise in Treasury yields documented by Nuveen in its March 30, 2026, commentary is consistent with this channel activating ([Nuveen Fixed Income Insights](https://www.nuveen.com/global/insights/fixed-income)). Nuveen's broader 2026 outlook characterizes the fixed income landscape as "more differentiated, policy-driven, and investable than in recent years," implying that monetary policy divergence across regions is adding complexity to this channel rather than simplifying it.

### The Compounding Effect

What makes the current environment particularly challenging is the simultaneity of these three channels. This is not merely an oil story. The energy shock compresses margins (Channel 1), erodes real incomes (Channel 2), andby keeping inflation elevatedprevents the monetary easing that would otherwise cushion equity valuations (Channel 3). Nuveen's observation that "EM and DM distinctions are blurring" and that "fiscal discipline and credit quality now matter more than just geography" underscores a further second-order effect: the stagflation transmission chain is globalizing, with traditional safe-haven assumptions breaking down as fiscal positions deteriorate across both developed and emerging markets ([Nuveen Fixed Income Insights](https://www.nuveen.com/global/insights/fixed-income)).

The precedent set during the UK's Moody's downgrade from Aa2 to Aa3 in October 2020a move that reflected fiscal deterioration even in a major developed economyillustrates how sovereign credit quality can erode under sustained macro stress ([TreasurySpring](https://treasuryspring.com/insights/less-negative-the-new-positive)). The interaction effectsmargin compression reducing corporate investment, consumer pullback amplifying recessionary signals, and monetary policy remaining restrictivecreate a compounding dynamic that is more severe than any single channel in isolation.

## The Highest-Signal Evidence: Yields Up, Spreads Out

The simultaneous rise in Treasury yields and widening of credit spreads across fixed income markets represents one of the most telling signals available to investors attempting to distinguish between healthy reflation and stagflationary deterioration. Nuveen, which manages $463 billion in fixed income assets, flagged this dynamic directly in its March 30, 2026 commentary, noting that "Middle East conflict drives oil prices and Treasury yields higher, while spreads widen across fixed income markets" ([Nuveen Fixed Income Insights](https://www.nuveen.com/global/insights/fixed-income)).

In a standard reflationary environment, rising yields typically accompany tightening spreads as improving economic conditions reduce perceived credit risk. The reversal of that patternyields up, spreads outsuggests markets are pricing in both inflation persistence and growing concern about borrowers' ability to service debt, a combination historically associated with stagflationary stress.

### The Mechanism

When geopolitical conflict pushes oil prices higher, it functions as a supply-side shock that raises input costs for businesses and consumers while simultaneously eroding real purchasing power. Treasury yields rise as inflation expectations adjust upward, but unlike demand-driven inflation, the underlying economic activity does not strengthen enough to improve corporate creditworthiness. The result is wider credit spreads, reflecting lenders' growing unease about default risk in an environment where revenues may stagnate even as financing costs climb.

Nuveen's broader 2026 outlook reinforces this reading, characterizing the global fixed income landscape as "more differentiated, policy-driven, and investable than in recent years"language that implicitly acknowledges a regime where traditional correlations are breaking down and selective credit analysis has become essential ([Nuveen Fixed Income Insights](https://www.nuveen.com/global/insights/fixed-income)).

### A Counter-Benchmark: The 2020 Reflation

To understand how sharply current conditions diverge from a healthy recovery, the Q3 2020 bank earnings cycle provides a usefulif imperfectcounter-benchmark. During that period, JP Morgan reported revenue of $29.94 billion against expectations of $28.44 billion, net income of $9.44 billion versus the $8.40 billion consensus, and a dramatic 94.18% decline in loan loss provisions relative to Q2 2020 ([TreasurySpring](https://treasuryspring.com/insights/less-negative-the-new-positive)). Goldman Sachs posted revenue of $10.78 billion against $9.45 billion expected, with net income of $3.62 billion representing a 92.55% increase year-over-year ([TreasurySpring](https://treasuryspring.com/insights/less-negative-the-new-positive)).

Those results reflected a classic reflationary dynamic: massive fiscal and monetary stimulus was flowing through the banking system, credit conditions were easing rapidly, and the sharp reduction in provisions signaled that banks believed the worst credit losses were behind them. Interest rates in Europe were deeply negative, with 3-Month Euribor printing consistently below the ECB's target rate and UK Treasury Bill returns reaching as low as -0.09% on the secondary market ([TreasurySpring](https://treasuryspring.com/insights/less-negative-the-new-positive)). The policy backdrop was unambiguously accommodative.

By contrast, the 2026 environment features rising yields driven not by recovery-era normalization but by supply-side inflation pressures, with credit spreads widening rather than compressingprecisely the opposite of what characterized the 2020 reflation.

### Evidence Gaps

The available Nuveen commentary identifies the directional trendsrising oil prices, rising yields, widening spreadsbut does not specify the magnitude of spread widening, which sectors are most affected, or the precise Treasury yield and oil price levels as of late March 2026. The nature and expected duration of the Middle East conflict referenced by Nuveen also remains underspecified. Without these details, it is difficult to assess whether current spread levels have reached historically significant thresholds or remain within normal fluctuation ranges. What can be stated with confidence is that the directional combinationyields and spreads moving in tandemis a qualitatively different signal from the spread compression and provision declines that defined the 2020 recovery.

## The Fed's Paralysis and the Repricing of Risk

The mechanical relationship between rate expectations and equity valuations is straightforward in theory but disorderly in practice. When the market reprices from two expected Fed cuts to oneor potentially nonethe risk-free rate anchor shifts upward, directly compressing the equity risk premium. In a standard discounted cash flow framework, a higher discount rate reduces the present value of future earnings, pressuring forward price-to-earnings multiples. This effect is most acute for long-duration equitiesgrowth stocks whose valuations depend heavily on earnings projected years into the future.

The current environment is further complicated by the exogenous supply shocks reinforcing the hawkish repricing. Rising energy costs feed directly into inflation expectations, making it harder for the Fed to justify rate cuts even if growth softens. This creates a particularly challenging environment for the equity risk premium: the numerator (expected equity returns) faces pressure from margin compression due to higher input costs, while the denominator (risk-free rate) remains elevated or rises further ([Nuveen Fixed Income Insights](https://www.nuveen.com/global/insights/fixed-income)).

Nuveen's emphasis on a "policy-driven" landscape is critical for understanding how the equity risk premium reprices in this regime. When Fed signaling dominates fundamentals, traditional valuation anchorsearnings growth, productivity gains, credit conditionstake a back seat to the parsing of dot plots, press conferences, and inflation data releases. Equity multiples become more volatile around policy events, and the risk premium investors demand for holding equities over bonds must compensate not just for earnings uncertainty but for policy uncertainty as well.

### Sector and Factor Implications

Stagflation creates a distinctive set of winners and losers across equity sectors and factor exposures. Historical episodes suggest that energy, commodities, and real assets tend to outperform because their revenues are directly linked to the rising input prices that define the environment. Value stocks with strong current cash flows and short-duration earnings profiles hold up better than growth stocks. Dividend aristocratscompanies with long track records of growing payoutsoffer a partial inflation hedge through rising nominal income streams.

Conversely, consumer discretionary names suffer as households' real purchasing power erodes, while unprofitable growth and high-leverage companies face a double squeeze: higher discount rates compress their multiples, and tighter credit conditions raise their funding costs. These are historical patterns; current-cycle dynamics can diverge depending on the specific policy mix and the nature of the supply shock.

Nuveen's observation that "EM and DM distinctions are blurring" and that fiscal discipline and credit quality now matter more than geography extends naturally to equity selection: balance sheet quality and pricing power matter more than sector labels alone ([Nuveen Fixed Income Insights](https://www.nuveen.com/global/insights/fixed-income)).

### The Rehabilitation of Cash

Perhaps the most underappreciated consequence of evaporating rate cuts is the rehabilitation of cash as a competitive asset class. When short-term rates remain elevated, money market funds and Treasury bills offer yields that meaningfully compete with the expected returns on riskier assetsparticularly when the equity risk premium is compressed. This is not a call for wholesale de-risking, but rather an acknowledgment that the opportunity cost of holding cash has declined substantially relative to the prior cycle. For institutional portfolios, maintaining a higher cash allocation provides optionality: the ability to deploy capital when the repricing creates dislocations, rather than being forced to ride out drawdowns in fully invested positions.

A note of caution is warranted when translating historical stagflation playbooks to the current cycle. The 1970s featured a very different labor market structure, energy dependence profile, and central bank credibility framework than today's economy. While the directional tiltstoward energy, value, real assets, and away from unprofitable growth and high leverageare supported by both historical precedent and the current macro evidence, the magnitude and duration of these rotations remain uncertain.

## Scenario Framework: Base Case, Upside, and Downside for 2026

The scenarios below map the range of plausible macro outcomes for the remainder of 2026, anchored in observable market dynamics as of late March. They are not forecasts but structured ways to think about risk allocation under genuine uncertainty. Probability weights are rough and reflect the balance of evidence available.

### Base Case (~50% Probability): Grinding Stagnation

Oil prices remain elevated in a $95$110 per barrel corridor, sustained by ongoing Middle East tensions that Nuveen's March 30, 2026 commentary already identified as driving yields and spreads higher ([Nuveen Fixed Income Insights](https://www.nuveen.com/global/insights/fixed-income)). Core PCE inflation drifts in a 2.83.2% rangetoo high for the Fed to cut, but not accelerating enough to force a hike. The Fed holds rates all year, and the equity risk premium expands by roughly 5075 basis points. Under these conditions, the S&P 500 forward multiple would likely contract to approximately 1718x. Credit spreads widen modestly but remain orderly, consistent with Nuveen's characterization of the landscape as "more differentiated, policy-driven, and investable than in recent years." Key assumptions: the Middle East conflict neither escalates sharply nor resolves, and the labor market softens without outright contraction.

### Upside Escape (~20% Probability): De-escalation and Disinflation

This scenario requires a meaningful reduction in geopolitical riskspecifically, a diplomatic resolution or sustained ceasefire in the Middle East that pushes oil prices below $90 per barrel. With energy costs falling, headline and core inflation would decelerate more quickly, potentially opening the door for the Fed to deliver one rate cut by Q4 2026. Growth stocks would likely recover as the term premium compresses. It is uncertain whether the geopolitical conditions for this scenario are realistic given the conflict dynamics described by Nuveen in late March 2026; the evidence currently points toward persistence rather than resolution. This scenario also assumes no new supply-side shocks from trade policy or other sources.

### Downside: 1970s Echo (~30% Probability): Oil Spike and Policy Trap

The Middle East conflict escalates further or spreads, pushing oil above $120 per barrel. Core PCE re-accelerates above 3.5%, and the Fed is forced into at least one additional rate hike despite weakening growtha classic stagflationary bind. Payrolls turn negative for two consecutive months. Credit spreads blow out as corporate borrowing costs surge and refinancing risk crystallizes, particularly in lower-quality segments. Nuveen's observation that fiscal discipline and credit quality now matter more than geography takes on sharper relevance in this environment, as indiscriminate spread widening gives way to severe differentiation between issuers ([Nuveen Fixed Income Insights](https://www.nuveen.com/global/insights/fixed-income)). Equity drawdowns of 20% or more from current levels become plausible.

### Caveats Across All Scenarios

The specific oil price levels referenced in Nuveen's March 30 commentary are not publicly detailed, and the exact magnitude of spread widening observed as of late March 2026 remains an open question. The probability weights (50/20/30) reflect a judgment that the distribution of risks is skewed to the downside given the current geopolitical backdrop, but these are inherently imprecise. The boundaries between scenarios are not rigid: a partial de-escalation that still leaves oil above $100 could produce a hybrid outcome blending elements of the base case and upside.

## What to Watch Next: Five Triggers That Confirm or Break the Stagflation Regime

The difference between a stagflationary episode and a temporary growth scare often becomes apparent only in hindsight. For investors navigating the current environment, a disciplined watchlist of leading indicators can help distinguish between the two in something closer to real time. No single data point will be dispositiveit is the interaction among these triggers, and the speed at which they reinforce one another, that will confirm or break the regime.

**Trigger 1: The Next Monthly Payrolls Print.** A single weak employment report can reflect seasonal noise. A second consecutive negative nonfarm payrolls reading would represent a qualitative shift, signaling that labor demand is contracting rather than merely cooling. Historically, consecutive negative prints have preceded formal recession declarations by the NBER. Watch not only the headline number but also revisions to prior months and the household survey's employment-to-population ratio. If payrolls deteriorate while inflation remains elevated, the Fed's dual mandate comes into direct internal conflict.

**Trigger 2: Core PCE Month-Over-Month Trajectory.** The Fed's preferred inflation gauge needs to be monitored at the monthly level because base effects can obscure underlying momentum. Any sustained acceleration above 0.3% month-over-month in core PCE would suggest inflationary pressures are becoming entrenched. This threshold annualizes to roughly 3.6%, well above the 2% target and inconsistent with a policy path toward easing. If core PCE accelerates even as growth stalls, the stagflation diagnosis strengthens considerably.

**Trigger 3: Oil Prices and Geopolitical Risk.** As of late March 2026, Nuveen's commentary noted that Middle East conflict was already driving oil prices and Treasury yields higher ([Nuveen Fixed Income Insights](https://www.nuveen.com/global/insights/fixed-income)). A sustained move in crude oil above $110 per barrel would act as a tax on consumption and corporate margins simultaneously. Conversely, a retreat below $90 on geopolitical de-escalation would relieve one of the most direct supply-side inflation channels. The precise oil price levels referenced in Nuveen's commentary are not specified, so the $110/$90 thresholds should be treated as illustrative benchmarks. What is clear is that the oil-Treasury yield-spread nexus is already active.

**Trigger 4: Fed Communication and the Dot Plot.** The next FOMC statement and Summary of Economic Projections will be scrutinized for any explicit acknowledgment of a stagflation tradeofflanguage that would represent a meaningful departure from the Fed's recent framing. If the Dot Plot shifts to show fewer rate cuts alongside downgraded growth forecasts, markets would likely interpret this as the Fed conceding that it cannot address both sides of its mandate simultaneously. Any such shift would be a powerful catalyst for repricing across duration-sensitive assets ([Nuveen Fixed Income Insights](https://www.nuveen.com/global/insights/fixed-income)).

**Trigger 5: Investment-Grade and High-Yield Credit Spreads.** The simultaneous widening of credit spreads and rising Treasury yields is arguably the clearest real-time stagflation signal available. In a normal risk-off episode, spreads widen but yields fall as investors seek safety in government bonds; in stagflation, both move adversely because inflation prevents the flight-to-quality dynamic from compressing sovereign yields. Nuveen's March 2026 commentary already flagged that spreads were widening alongside rising yields ([Nuveen Fixed Income Insights](https://www.nuveen.com/global/insights/fixed-income)). If high-yield spreads breach levels consistent with prior stress episodes while investment-grade spreads follow, the credit market would effectively be pricing in both slower growth and persistent inflation simultaneously.

The overarching point is that these five indicators form a system, not a checklist. Oil price shocks feed into core PCE; labor market deterioration widens credit spreads; the Fed's response to conflicting signals reshapes the yield curve and risk premia. A stagflation regime is confirmed not by any single trigger firing but by multiple triggers reinforcing one another in a self-sustaining loop.

## Conclusion

The evidence as of late March 2026 points to a macroeconomic environment where **stagflation** has moved from a background risk to a foreground reality for asset allocators. The transmission chain is active across all three channelsenergy-driven margin compression, real wage erosion feeding consumer pullback, and sticky inflation preventing the monetary easing that would otherwise cushion valuations. Nuveen's observation that Middle East conflict is simultaneously driving oil prices, Treasury yields, and credit spreads higher captures the core dynamic: a supply-side shock that tightens financial conditions while weakening growth, leaving the Federal Reserve with no clean policy option ([Nuveen Fixed Income Insights](https://www.nuveen.com/global/insights/fixed-income)).

The blurring of traditional market categoriesemerging versus developed, investment-grade versus high-yield, growth versus valuefurther complicates the landscape. Fiscal discipline, credit quality, and pricing power have become the primary differentiators, regardless of geography or sector label. The rehabilitation of cash as a competitive asset class, the defensive imperative of shorter duration, and the tilt toward real assets and cash-flow-rich equities all follow logically from this regime.

Important uncertainties remain. The specific magnitude of the oil price shock, the precise extent of spread widening, and the duration of the Middle East conflict are not fully detailed in available sources. Whether the current episode resolves into grinding stagnation, a disinflationary escape, or a deeper 1970s-style policy trap depends on geopolitical developments and central bank choices that are genuinely unpredictable. Investors should treat the sector and factor tilts described here as probabilistic leanings grounded in evidence, not deterministic outcomes, and should calibrate position sizes to reflect the genuine uncertainty that a stagflationary environment entails. The watchlist of triggerspayrolls, core PCE momentum, oil prices, Fed communication, and the yields-plus-spreads signaloffers the most practical framework for distinguishing in real time between a temporary scare and a durable regime shift.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is stagflation and why is it different from a normal recession or inflation spike?**

Stagflation is the simultaneous occurrence of persistent inflation, stalling economic growth, and rising unemployment. It differs from a normal recession (where inflation typically falls as demand weakens) and from a standard inflation spike (where growth is usually strong). The defining feature is that monetary policy tools designed to fight inflationraising or holding rates highdirectly worsen the employment picture, while tools to support growthcutting ratesrisk unanchoring inflation expectations. This internal conflict within the Fed's dual mandate is what makes stagflation uniquely difficult for policymakers and investors alike.

**Which equity sectors historically perform best during stagflationary periods?**

Historical episodes, most notably the 1970s, suggest that energy, commodities, and real assets tend to outperform because their revenues are directly linked to the rising input prices that define the environment. Value stocks with strong current cash flows and short-duration earnings profiles hold up better than growth stocks. Dividend aristocrats offer a partial inflation hedge through rising nominal income streams. Conversely, consumer discretionary, unprofitable growth, and high-leverage companies tend to underperform. These are historical patterns; current-cycle dynamics can diverge depending on the specific policy mix and supply shock.

**How does the Fed's dual mandate create policy paralysis during stagflation?**

The Fed is mandated to pursue both price stability and maximum employment. In most economic environments, these goals are complementarycutting rates supports jobs and eventually stabilizes prices, or raising rates cools inflation without excessive employment damage. In stagflation, the goals conflict directly: raising rates to fight inflation accelerates job losses, while cutting rates to support employment risks further entrenching inflation. This paralysis removes a key source of downside protection that equity investors have relied upon in every downturn since 2008.

**What happens to growth stocks when rate cut expectations collapse?**

When the market reprices from multiple expected rate cuts to fewer or none, the risk-free rate used to discount future cash flows rises. Growth stocks, whose valuations depend heavily on earnings projected years into the future, are disproportionately affected because a larger share of their present value comes from distant cash flows. The repricing is amplified by behavioral dynamics as portfolio managers unwind duration bets simultaneously. Cash-flow-rich value stocks, by contrast, derive more of their present value from near-term earnings and are relatively less sensitive to discount rate changes.

**How does oil above $100 transmit into corporate earnings and consumer spending simultaneously?**

Rising oil prices increase input costs for energy-intensive businessesairlines, industrials, consumer-facing firmscompressing margins before companies can reprice their output. Simultaneously, higher energy and food prices erode real household purchasing power, particularly for lower- and middle-income consumers whose spending is weighted toward essentials. The resulting pullback in discretionary spending feeds back into corporate revenues and eventually payrolls, creating a negative feedback loop that is the hallmark of stagflationary dynamics. As Nuveen noted in March 2026, this dynamic was already widening credit spreads as markets priced in deteriorating fundamentals ([Nuveen Fixed Income Insights](https://www.nuveen.com/global/insights/fixed-income)).

**Is the current stagflation risk comparable to the 1970s or is this a milder episode?**

The directional pressuressupply-side energy shock, sticky inflation, constrained central bankrhyme with the 1970s, but the structural context differs. Today's economy has a different labor market structure, lower energy intensity relative to GDP, and a central bank with greater credibility than the pre-Volcker Fed. The 1970s also featured multiple sequential oil shocks and a prolonged period of policy error. Whether the current episode remains a milder "stagflationary tilt" or deepens into something more severe depends on the duration of the Middle East conflict and the Fed's response to incoming datafactors that remain genuinely uncertain.

**How should investors reposition portfolios if stagflation persists through 2026?**

The evidence supports tilting toward shorter duration in both equity and fixed income allocations, favoring companies with near-term cash flow generation and pricing power. Real assets, including commodities and infrastructure, regain relevance as partial hedges against inflation persistence. Cash becomes a more competitive asset class when short-term rates remain elevated, providing optionality to deploy capital during dislocations. Credit-by-credit analysis replaces broad geographic or sector allocation as the primary risk management tool, consistent with Nuveen's observation that fiscal discipline and credit quality now matter more than geography ([Nuveen Fixed Income Insights](https://www.nuveen.com/global/insights/fixed-income)).

**What are the most reliable leading indicators that stagflation is worsening or easing?**

Five indicators form an interconnected system: (1) consecutive negative payrolls prints signaling labor market contraction; (2) core PCE month-over-month readings sustained above 0.3%, suggesting entrenched inflation; (3) oil pricessustained moves above $110 intensify stagflationary pressure, while retreats below $90 relieve it; (4) Fed communication, particularly any Dot Plot shift showing fewer cuts alongside downgraded growth forecasts; and (5) the simultaneous widening of credit spreads and rising Treasury yields, which is the clearest real-time market signal of stagflation. The convergence of multiple triggers matters more than any individual reading.
