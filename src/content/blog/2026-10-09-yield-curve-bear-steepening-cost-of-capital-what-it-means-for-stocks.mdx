# How a bear steepening drives up the cost of capital

When the yield curve undergoes a bear steepening, long-term interest rates rise faster than short-term rates. When this move is driven by expanding term premiums rather than accelerating economic growth, it raises financing costs across the economy. For equities, higher benchmark yields elevate discount rates, compress valuation multiples, and narrow the excess return investors demand over risk-free government debt.

### Anatomy of the move: term premiums and benchmark yields

A bear steepening occurs when yields on longer-dated bonds climb faster than yields on short-dated paper. This dynamic was evident when the U.S. 10-year Treasury note yield rose to its highest level since 2002 ahead of a bond sale, as reported by [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/markets).

Much of this move stems from an expanding term premium, which represents the extra yield investors demand to hold duration and interest rate risk over multi-year horizons. While yield curve steepening often reflects faster economic activity, shifts driven by term premiums work differently. The [Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/may-2021-asset-valuations.htm) noted that an increase in term premiums, if unaccompanied by a strengthening of the economic outlook, can exert downward pressure on valuations across asset classes.

When long-term yields advance due to higher risk compensation rather than upgraded earnings expectations, discount rates rise without an offsetting lift to future corporate cash flows, depressing asset prices.

### Squeezing the equity risk buffer

Higher benchmark yields compress the risk buffer in equity markets. The S&P 500 Equity Risk Premium, measured as the index's earnings yield minus the 10-year Treasury yield, dropped to its lowest level in a quarter century, according to market data from [Barchart](https://www.facebook.com/Barchart/posts/sp-500-equity-risk-premium-earnings-yield-minus-10-year-treasury-yield-is-at-its/1432061555569139). Earlier in the tightening cycle, this spread had fallen to a 14-year low, reaching levels not seen since 2008, as reported by [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/analysis/real-yields-spike-and-the-equity-risk-premium-plunges-200630301).

When default-risk-free government bond yields climb, stocks look relatively more expensive for investors [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/analysis/real-yields-spike-and-the-equity-risk-premium-plunges-200630301). Equity investors accept narrow excess returns relative to risk-free debt. When discount rates rise without stronger growth expectations, projected cash flows lose present value under higher discount factors, pressuring price-to-earnings multiples.

Available market evidence does not provide granular breakdowns showing the exact pace or dispersion of valuation adjustments across specific growth sectors.

### Frequently asked questions

**How does a bear steepener specifically differ from a bull steepener in its effect on equity discount rates?**
In a bear steepener, long-term yields rise faster than short-term yields, which pushes long-term discount rates higher and increases the baseline cost of capital. In contrast, a bull steepener occurs when short-term yields fall more rapidly than long-term yields, typically because of monetary easing, which lowers short-term borrowing costs and reduces front-end discount rates across the economy.
