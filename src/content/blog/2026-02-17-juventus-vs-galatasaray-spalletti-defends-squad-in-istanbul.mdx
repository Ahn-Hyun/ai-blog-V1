### The Istanbul Gauntlet: A Question of Courage

The trip to Rams Park in Istanbul is widely regarded as one of the most intimidating fixtures in world football. For **Juventus**, this is not merely a logistical challenge but a psychological examination. The stakes of the Champions League playoffs require a mental fortitude that has been questioned intermittently throughout the 2025-26 campaign. According to recent reports, Spalletti has emphasized that "tonight requires the right courage," explicitly challenging his squad to rise above the environmental hostility.

The tactical setup for this encounter is dominated by the presence of Victor Osimhen in the Galatasaray attack. The Nigerian striker, currently on loan and thriving in Turkey, presents a unique paradox for Spalletti. Having coached Osimhen to a Scudetto at Napoli, the Juventus manager knows the striker's movements better than anyone. However, knowledge does not always equate to control. In a recent press conference, Spalletti assured the media that [Juventus defenders have the capacity to contain him](https://www.sportmediaset.mediaset.it/calcio/juventus-spalletti-stasera-serve-il-coraggio-giusto-osimhen-i-nostri-centrali-possono-contenerlo_109135386-202602k.shtml), signaling a reliance on anticipation rather than physical duels. The strategy appears to be cutting off the supply lines rather than engaging in a footrace with one of the world's fastest forwards.

### The Spalletti Shield: Defending Pierre Kalulu

While the focus should be on the pitch, a significant media storm has erupted involving defender Pierre Kalulu. The controversy stems from comments made by Cristian Chivu, the former Inter defender and current pundit, who allegedly questioned Kalulu's intelligence or decision-making on the pitch. Spalletti's reaction was visceral and protective, a trademark of his management style when he feels his "men" are under unfair attack.

![Spalletti Press Conference](https://encrypted-tbn0.gstatic.com/images?q=tbn:ANd9GcRGjY9BUv6vUbfZvUUAlyT5w2S8DTSFJfc80hX9woKyDwJujJjmOa39TtgzjzM)

Spalletti's rebuttal was sharp. He claimed that Kalulu has suffered "colossal wrongs"likely referring to officiating decisions or previous tactical mismanagementand warned pundits not to dare call him a fool. As reported by [Tuttosport](https://www.tuttosport.com/news/calcio/serie-a/juventus/2026/02/16-146651431/spalletti_schiaffo_a_chivu_kalulu_ha_subito_torti_colossali_non_si_permetta_di_dargli_del_fesso), Spalletti stated, "He has suffered colossal wrongs, do not allow yourself to call him a fool." This public dressing down of Chivu serves a dual purpose. Firstly, it shields Kalulu from confidence-eroding criticism right before a massive European tie. Secondly, it creates a siege mentality, uniting the dressing room against external detractors. In the high-pressure environment of Turin, where the media cycle is relentless, Spalletti is attempting to deflect the heat from his players onto himself.

### The **Juventus** Tactical Dilemma: Containing Osimhen

The central tactical question remains: How do you stop a force of nature like Osimhen when you are playing away from home? Spalletti's confidence in his central defenders is notable, but the data suggests caution. Galatasaray's home form in the 2025-2026 season has been imperious, largely driven by their aggressive start to matches.

If Juventus sits too deep, they invite shots from distance and set-piece situations, an area where Galatasaray excels physically. If they push too high, Osimhen's pace becomes lethal. The key may lie in the midfield battle. By dominating possession and slowing the tempo, Juventus can silence the Rams Park crowd and starve the Galatasaray attack of service. This requires a level of technical precision and "courage" that Spalletti has demanded. The psychological aspect of Kalulu's performance will be critical here; following the public defense by his manager, the defender will be under immense pressure to deliver a flawless performance.
