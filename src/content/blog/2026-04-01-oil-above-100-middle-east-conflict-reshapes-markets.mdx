# Oil Above $100: Middle East Conflict, Widening Spreads, and the Earnings Disconnect Reshaping Markets

When Nuveenmanager of [$463 billion in fixed income assets](https://www.nuveen.com/global/insights/fixed-income)titles its weekly commentary "Rising oil prices test fixed income resilience" and warns that "Middle East conflict drives oil prices and Treasury yields higher, while spreads widen across fixed income markets," the signal is difficult to dismiss as noise. The oil-above-100-middle-east-conflict dynamic now unfolding is not simply an energy story. It is a macro repricing event that touches every asset class, every borrower, and every portfolio allocation decision.

The simultaneous upward pressure on yields and outward pressure on credit spreads describes a transmission mechanism that reaches well beyond the energy complex. Higher oil prices feed directly into headline inflation readings, which in turn constrain the Federal Reserve's ability to ease monetary policy. The result is a macro environment where growth expectations soften even as the cost of capital risesthe textbook definition of a stagflationary impulse. This is the backdrop against which every asset class must now be re-evaluated.

What makes the current episode analytically significant is not the geopolitical headline itselfthose are noisy and unpredictablebut the measurable second-order effects already visible in the data. Over the 60 days ending March 30, 2026, cumulative 2026 earnings estimates for the top 30 weighted stocks in the Oil & Gas Equipment & Services ETF (XES) declined by 1.80%, while 2027 estimates fell by 2.72%, according to [Hedge Fund Tips' revision tracker](https://www.hedgefundtips.com/oil-gas-equipment-services-earnings-estimates-and-revisions-13/). The revision ratio was 13 upward to 15 downwardmore analysts cutting numbers than raising them. If the obvious beneficiaries of higher oil prices are not benefiting at the earnings level, what does that imply for sectors with pure cost exposure to energy?

This article traces that implication through credit markets, equity valuations, and portfolio positioning. It maps the transmission chain from oil prices to financial conditions, examines the highest-signal data points from late March 2026, identifies winners and losers across the value chain, and lays out a scenario framework with concrete triggers for what to watch next.

## The Transmission Chain: From Oil Shock to Financial Stress

The transmission mechanism from rising oil prices to broader financial-market stress operates through at least three distinct but interconnected channels, all of which appear to be activating simultaneously as of late March 2026. Nuveen's fixed income weekly commentary, published on March 30, 2026, explicitly identifies the linkage: Middle East conflict is driving oil prices and Treasury yields higher, while spreads are widening across fixed income markets ([Nuveen Fixed Income Insights](https://www.nuveen.com/global/insights/fixed-income)). This simultaneous movement across asset classesoil, sovereign yields, and credit spreadsis the hallmark of a supply-side shock propagating through the financial system.

While the precise level of oil prices and the magnitude of yield and spread moves are not specified in the available evidence, the directional co-movement is itself significant. Markets are repricing not just energy costs but the broader inflation and policy outlook.

### Channel One: Inflation Pass-Through

When oil prices rise sharply due to geopolitical supply disruption rather than demand strength, the effect flows into headline CPI through gasoline and energy costs, and into core-adjacent categories through transportation, logistics, petrochemical inputs, and food production. This matters for monetary policy because the Federal Reserve's rate-cut calculus depends heavily on the inflation trajectory.

A supply-driven oil shock pushes headline inflation higher without corresponding economic strength, forcing the Fed into an uncomfortable position: cutting rates to support growth risks entrenching inflation expectations, while holding rates steady or tightening amplifies the contractionary drag. Nuveen's observation that Treasury yields are rising alongside oil prices is consistent with markets repricing the path of Fed policyspecifically, reducing expectations for near-term rate cuts as inflation risks increase ([Nuveen Fixed Income Insights](https://www.nuveen.com/global/insights/fixed-income)).

### Channel Two: The Earnings Channel

The earnings channel manifests through margin compression in energy-consuming sectors and, paradoxically, even within parts of the energy industry itself. Data from Hedge Fund Tips tracking the top 30 weighted stocks in the Oil & Gas Equipment & Services ETF (XES) shows that cumulative 2026 earnings power declined by 1.80% and 2027 estimates fell by 2.72% over the 60 days ending March 30, 2026, with more estimates revised downward (15) than upward (13) ([Hedge Fund Tips  OFS Earnings Estimates](https://www.hedgefundtips.com/oil-gas-equipment-services-earnings-estimates-and-revisions-13/)).

This is a counterintuitive result. Rising oil prices would typically be expected to benefit oilfield services companies through increased upstream activity. The sources do not reconcile this tension directly, and it remains uncertain whether the downward revisions reflect cost pressures within the OFS sector, expectations of demand destruction at sustained high prices, geopolitical constraints on production activity, or other industry-specific headwinds. What is clear is that even sectors nominally positioned to benefit from higher oil are not uniformly seeing earnings upgrades, which complicates the investment case and suggests the earnings channel is more broadly negative than a simple sectoral rotation would imply.

### Channel Three: Financial Conditions

The third channel is the mechanism through which rising yields and widening spreads tighten the credit environment for corporate borrowers. When Treasury yields rise because markets expect the Fed to hold rates higher for longer, corporate borrowing costs increase mechanically, since investment-grade and high-yield bonds are priced as a spread over the risk-free rate. Simultaneously, if investors perceive that higher energy costs will compress margins and increase default risk, credit spreads widen on top of the higher base rate.

Nuveen titled its March 30 commentary "Rising oil prices test fixed income resilience," language that signals the stress is material enough to warrant institutional attention ([Nuveen Fixed Income Insights](https://www.nuveen.com/global/insights/fixed-income)). The widening of spreads noted in that commentary indicates that the market is not merely adjusting for a higher risk-free rate but is also repricing credit risk itself.

### The Stagflationary Feedback Loop

The reason these three channels create a particularly difficult policy problem is that they fire simultaneously and reinforce each other. Higher oil prices push inflation up (channel one), compress corporate earnings (channel two), and tighten financial conditions (channel three). In a demand-driven inflation shock, the Fed can raise rates with some confidence that cooling demand will eventually reduce both inflation and overheating. In a supply-driven shock, however, the economy is already weakening from the cost side, and tighter monetary policy compounds the contractionary effect without addressing the root causea geopolitical supply disruption.

Nuveen's early 2026 outlook described the global fixed income landscape as ["more differentiated, policy-driven, and investable than in recent years,"](https://www.nuveen.com/global/insights/fixed-income) but the March developments suggest that policy-driven differentiation cuts both ways: fiscal discipline and credit quality now matter more than geography, and markets are actively discriminating against borrowers and sovereigns perceived as vulnerable to the oil-inflation-rates nexus.

It is important to note the limits of the available evidence. The specific price of oil, the magnitude of yield increases, and the breadth of spread widening are not quantified in the sources reviewed. The apparent contradiction between rising oil prices and declining OFS earnings estimates remains unresolved. Nevertheless, the directional evidenceoil up, yields up, spreads wider, earnings estimates downis consistent across independent sources and points to a transmission chain already in motion.

## The Highest-Signal Data: What March 30 Actually Told Us

The final trading day of March 2026 produced two data points that, taken together, frame the central tension in energy-linked markets.

On March 30, Nuveen published its weekly commentary under the title "Rising oil prices test fixed income resilience," stating plainly that ["Middle East conflict drives oil prices and Treasury yields higher, while spreads widen across fixed income markets."](https://www.nuveen.com/global/insights/fixed-income) This was not a speculative warning but a description of conditions already in progress. The commentary's framing as a test of "resilience" implies that Nuveen's analysts viewed the oil-driven pressure as material enough to challenge portfolio positioning across sectors. The precise level of oil prices and the magnitude of spread widening were not specified in the available summary, and those details remain important open questions.

Nuveen's broader 2026 outlook, published on or before March 9, had already described the global fixed income landscape as ["more differentiated, policy-driven, and investable than in recent years,"](https://www.nuveen.com/global/insights/fixed-income) with a specific emphasis on the blurring of emerging-market and developed-market distinctions. The March 30 weekly note, arriving roughly three weeks later, effectively updated that framework with a concrete stress event: oil-driven inflation feeding into Treasury yields and credit spreads simultaneously. When a fixed income manager of Nuveen's scale publicly names a specific geopolitical catalyst as the driver of spread widening, it signals that the thesis has moved from analyst speculation to institutional consensusor at minimum, to a positioning-relevant baseline.

### The OFS Earnings Puzzle

On the same date, Hedge Fund Tips published earnings-estimate revision data for the top 30 weighted stocks in the SPDR S&P Oil & Gas Equipment & Services ETF (XES), covering a 60-day lookback period beginning January 29, 2026. The results were unambiguously negative: the up-to-down revision ratio for 2026 earnings estimates was 13:15, meaning more analyst estimates were revised downward than upward. Cumulative 2026 earnings power for the group declined by 1.80%, and 2027 estimates fell by 2.72% over the same window. As the source summarized: ["What this table is telling us is that estimates have gone DOWN. More estimates came DOWN in the past 60 days than went UP."](https://www.hedgefundtips.com/oil-gas-equipment-services-earnings-estimates-and-revisions-13/)

This is where the data creates a genuine analytical puzzle. Rising oil prices, particularly those driven by supply-side disruption from geopolitical conflict, would conventionally be expected to benefit oilfield services companies by increasing upstream activity and pricing power. Yet the earnings-revision data shows the opposite.

Several hypotheses are plausible but remain unverified. Cost inflation within the OFS supply chainsteel, labor, logisticscould be compressing margins even as top-line activity rises. Alternatively, E&P operators may be exercising capex discipline, choosing to return cash to shareholders rather than expand drilling programs despite higher crude prices. A third possibility is simple lag: analyst models may not yet have incorporated the most recent price moves, particularly if the sharpest oil rally occurred in the final weeks of March.

It is important to distinguish what is established from what is inferred. The facts are that Nuveen identified oil-driven spread widening and yield pressure as of March 30 ([Nuveen](https://www.nuveen.com/global/insights/fixed-income)), and that OFS earnings estimates declined over the preceding 60 days on a 13:15 up-to-down ratio ([Hedge Fund Tips](https://www.hedgefundtips.com/oil-gas-equipment-services-earnings-estimates-and-revisions-13/)). The inferencethat these two data points are causally connected, or that the OFS estimate declines reflect a specific mechanism like cost inflation or capex restraintis not supported by either source. The conventional narrativehigher oil prices lift all energy boatswas not holding in late March 2026, at least not at the earnings-estimate level for oilfield services. That tension, rather than any single data point, is what makes this moment analytically significant.

## Winners, Losers, and the Intra-Sector Divide

### Who Benefits: E&Ps, Integrated Majors, and the Quality Factor

When oil prices rise above $100 per barrel, the most direct beneficiaries are exploration and production companies, whose revenues are mechanically tied to the commodity price. Each dollar increase in realized crude pricing flows almost entirely to the top line for producers with fixed or slowly adjusting cost structures, expanding operating margins significantly.

Refiners represent a second tier of potential winners, though their gains are conditional on the behavior of crack spreadsthe difference between the price of refined products and the cost of crude oil feedstock. When product demand remains firm or supply disruptions disproportionately affect crude rather than refined product inventories, crack spreads widen and refiner margins expand. Conversely, if crude prices spike faster than product prices can adjust, refiners can see margin compression even as headline oil prices climb. The current environment, where geopolitical conflict is the primary driver rather than a demand-side boom, introduces uncertainty about whether crack spreads will sustainably widen.

Integrated majorscompanies spanning upstream production, midstream transport, and downstream refiningoccupy a particularly favorable position. Their diversified revenue streams and typically conservative balance sheets generate excess free cash flow that management teams can deploy toward shareholder returns. In recent commodity upcycles, these companies have prioritized buybacks and dividend increases over aggressive capital spending, a pattern known as capex discipline. This capital allocation strategy is especially attractive when rising Treasury yields and widening credit spreadsboth confirmed by [Nuveen's March 2026 commentary](https://www.nuveen.com/global/insights/fixed-income)make leveraged growth equities less appealing.

This factor rotation thesis aligns with the broader dynamics of a stagflationary regime. Nuveen's 2026 outlook noted that ["fiscal discipline and credit quality now matter more than just geography in fixed income,"](https://www.nuveen.com/global/insights/fixed-income) a principle that extends logically to equity selection. Companies with strong balance sheets, high free cash flow yields, and limited refinancing risk become natural safe havens.

However, the benefits of high oil prices are not distributed uniformly across the energy value chain. The XES data15 downward estimate revisions versus only 13 upward, with cumulative 2026 earnings power declining by 1.80% ([Hedge Fund Tips](https://www.hedgefundtips.com/oil-gas-equipment-services-earnings-estimates-and-revisions-13/))suggests that the "rising tide lifts all boats" narrative does not hold within energy subsectors. The primary beneficiaries of oil above $100 are likely concentrated among low-cost producers and capital-disciplined integrated majors rather than the service companies that depend on increased drilling activity.

### Who Gets Hurt: Airlines, Chemicals, Trucking, and Leveraged Borrowers

The stagflationary pincer facing oil-sensitive sectors is defined by two simultaneous forces: rising input costs driven by geopolitical conflict and tightening financial conditions that compound the margin damage.

Airlines represent perhaps the most direct casualty of rising crude prices, as jet fuel typically constitutes 2030% of operating costs for major carriers. In a slowing demand environmentwhich wider spreads and rising yields tend to signalairlines face the worst of both worlds: fuel cost surges that hedging programs can only partially offset and fare increases that passengers in a weakening economy are unlikely to absorb. It should be noted that specific airline earnings revision data for this period is not available in the sourced evidence; the margin compression dynamic described here is inferred from the confirmed oil price trajectory and spread-widening environment rather than directly sourced sector-level estimates.

The chemicals sector faces an analogous feedstock cost problem. Petrochemical producers that rely on naphtha, ethane, and other hydrocarbon inputs see their cost curves shift upward when crude prices rise, and the ability to pass those costs through depends heavily on downstream demand elasticity. In a period where Treasury yields are climbing and credit spreads are wideningconditions Nuveen describes as testing "fixed income resilience" ([Nuveen Fixed Income Insights](https://www.nuveen.com/global/insights/fixed-income))end-market demand for plastics, coatings, and industrial chemicals is likely softening simultaneously.

Trucking and logistics operators face a parallel squeeze through diesel price inflation. Diesel costs are a primary variable expense for trucking fleets, and while fuel surcharges are a standard contractual mechanism, they typically lag spot price movements and rarely capture the full increase during rapid run-ups. The compounding factor is that freight volumes tend to contract when consumer spending weakensprecisely the outcome one would expect when gasoline prices erode household purchasing power.

The financial dimension of this pain is amplified by the credit spread widening that Nuveen documents. For leveraged companies in airlines, chemicals, trucking, and retail, wider spreads mean higher refinancing costs on existing debt, arriving at precisely the moment when operating cash flows are being compressed by rising input costs. Companies carrying significant debt loads in these energy-sensitive sectors face a dual margin squeeze: deteriorating operating margins from commodity costs and deteriorating financial margins from higher interest expenses.

A significant limitation must be noted: the only sector-specific earnings revision data sourced covers Oil & Gas Equipment & Services (XES). Comparable revision data for airlines, chemicals, trucking, or consumer discretionary sectors has not been located in the available sources. The earnings damage described in those sectors is an analytical inference based on confirmed macroeconomic conditions rather than a directly observed data point.

### Portfolio Positioning: Quality Over Momentum

The portfolio-positioning framework that emerges is one of selective defense rather than broad risk-off. Leveraged growth companiestypically found in technology, speculative biotech, and capital-intensive disruptorsrely on access to inexpensive debt and patient equity capital willing to discount distant cash flows at low rates. When Treasury yields rise and credit spreads widen simultaneously, both legs of the vise tighten concurrently.

Energy equities, often cited as a direct stagflation hedge, present a more nuanced picture. The OFS earnings revision dataa 13:15 up-to-down ratio with declining cumulative estimates ([Hedge Fund Tips](https://www.hedgefundtips.com/oil-gas-equipment-services-earnings-estimates-and-revisions-13/))demonstrates that investors treating energy as a monolithic stagflation hedge should note the intra-sector dispersion carefully.

The quality factoremphasizing balance sheet strength, return on equity, and earnings consistencyshould be preferred over momentum or pure growth factors in the current regime. Utilities, consumer staples, and healthcaresectors characterized by relatively inelastic demand and predictable cash flowshistorically outperform in stagflationary regimes precisely because their earnings are less sensitive to both the growth slowdown and the credit tightening that define such periods.

The key insight is that stagflation does not reward passive sector bets; it rewards granular analysis of which companies within each sector can sustain margins and service debt when the macro tide turns hostile. Nuveen's framing of a "more differentiated" landscape, backed by $463 billion in fixed income assets under management, lends institutional weight to this bottom-up, quality-first approach ([Nuveen Fixed Income Insights](https://www.nuveen.com/global/insights/fixed-income)).

## Three Scenarios: Base Case, Upside, and Downside

The scenario framework below is built on observable conditions as of late March 2026. It is important to emphasize at the outset that geopolitical outcomes are inherently unpredictable, and the precise spread, yield, and price levels used in these scenarios are illustrative rather than evidence-based forecasts. The available data confirms directional trendsrising oil, widening spreads, softening OFS earningsbut does not support pinpoint numerical targets.

### Base Case: Oil Sustains $100110, Fed Delays Cuts to H2 2026

The key assumption is that the Middle East conflict persists at roughly its current intensity without significant escalation or resolution, keeping oil elevated but not at crisis levels. Nuveen's observation that spreads are already widening and Treasury yields are rising is consistent with a scenario in which the Fed faces an uncomfortable trade-off between inflation pressure from energy costs and slowing growth ([Nuveen, 30 Mar 2026](https://www.nuveen.com/global/insights/fixed-income)).

In this environment, energy equities could plausibly outperform the broader market, though the magnitude of outperformance lacks direct evidentiary support and should be treated as uncertain. The weakest link is the assumption that oil remains range-bound: supply disruptions or demand shifts could easily push prices outside the $100110 corridor. The downward revision in OFS earnings estimates despite rising oil prices introduces an additional puzzlesuggesting that higher crude does not automatically translate into improved fundamentals across the energy services supply chain ([Hedge Fund Tips, 30 Mar 2026](https://www.hedgefundtips.com/oil-gas-equipment-services-earnings-estimates-and-revisions-13/)).

### Upside (for Risk Assets): Ceasefire Pulls Oil Below $90

The key assumption is that a resolution to the Middle East conflict is achievable in the near term and that the resulting oil price decline would be swift enough to meaningfully alter the inflation outlook. If oil retreats below $90, the pressure on Treasury yields that Nuveen has flagged would ease, potentially compressing the credit spreads that have been widening. Growth and technology equitiessectors most sensitive to rate expectationswould likely re-lead the market.

The weakest link is the diplomatic assumption itself: there is no publicly available evidence as of late March 2026 suggesting that a ceasefire is imminent. Additionally, even if oil falls, the Fed's reaction function may be slower than markets hope, given that Nuveen's 2026 outlook describes a landscape shaped by multiple competing factors beyond energy prices alone ([Nuveen, 30 Mar 2026](https://www.nuveen.com/global/insights/fixed-income)).

### Downside: Escalation Pushes Oil to $120+

The key assumption is a material escalation in the Middle East conflictsuch as direct confrontation between major oil-producing states or disruption to critical shipping lanesthat sends crude sharply higher. In this scenario, the spread widening already observed would intensify dramatically. The OFS earnings revision data adds a cautionary note: even before any hypothetical escalation, the up-to-down ratio for 2026 OFS earnings revisions stood at 13:15 ([Hedge Fund Tips, 30 Mar 2026](https://www.hedgefundtips.com/oil-gas-equipment-services-earnings-estimates-and-revisions-13/)). If oil surges further, the tension between rising crude prices and deteriorating service-sector fundamentals could widen, suggesting that not all energy subsectors would benefit uniformly.

The weakest link is the assumption that $120+ oil necessarily triggers a recession; the relationship between oil prices and economic contraction depends on the duration of the price shock, the pass-through to consumers, and the fiscal and monetary policy response.

Across all three scenarios, a common thread is that fiscal discipline and credit quality matter more than broad geographic or sectoral labelsa point Nuveen makes explicitly ([Nuveen, 30 Mar 2026](https://www.nuveen.com/global/insights/fixed-income)). The conflict between rising oil prices and declining OFS earnings estimates remains unresolved and represents a genuine analytical gap. Investors should treat the numerical thresholds in these scenarios as rough guideposts rather than calibrated forecasts.

## What to Watch Next: Five Triggers for the Weeks Ahead

The oil-credit-equity nexus is transmitting signals across asset classes in real time, and investors need a structured watchlist to separate noise from meaningful regime shifts. The following five triggers offer concrete, measurable indicators grounded in the dynamics already visible in the data.

**Trigger 1: WTI Front-Month Contract vs. $100.** The psychological and technical significance of the $100 per barrel level makes it a natural line of demarcation. A sustained close above $100 would amplify the inflationary pressures that Nuveen's March 30 commentary already identifies ([Nuveen Fixed Income Insights](https://www.nuveen.com/global/insights/fixed-income)). A sustained retreat below $100 would suggest the geopolitical risk premium is fading. The exact level of oil prices as of late March 2026 is not specified in available sources, so readers should track the front-month contract daily for directional confirmation.

**Trigger 2: Fed Funds Futures Implied Probability for June/September 2026 Cuts.** Rate expectations are the transmission mechanism between oil-driven inflation and credit market pricing. A sharp decline in the probability of June or September cuts would signal that the market is pricing in a higher-for-longer regime, cascading into wider credit spreads and lower equity multiples. Nuveen's 2026 outlook underscores that policy decisionsnot just macro fundamentalsare the dominant driver of fixed income returns ([Nuveen Fixed Income Insights](https://www.nuveen.com/global/insights/fixed-income)).

**Trigger 3: CDX Investment Grade and High Yield Spread LevelsWeekly Changes.** Credit default swap indices are among the fastest-moving barometers of systemic stress. Weekly changes of more than 1015 basis points in CDX IG, or 3050 basis points in CDX HY, would suggest credit markets are beginning to price in default risk rather than merely adjusting for rate expectations. Pay particular attention to whether energy-sector names are driving the widening or whether the stress is broadening into other industries, which would indicate contagion.

**Trigger 4: Q1 2026 Earnings Calls from Major Airlines and E&P Names.** Real-time margin commentary from fuel-intensive consumers alongside upstream producers will provide ground-truth data on how the oil shock is flowing through corporate income statements. The tension between rising oil prices and declining OFS earnings estimatescumulative 2026 earnings power down 1.80%, revision ratio 13 upward versus 15 downward ([Hedge Fund Tips](https://www.hedgefundtips.com/oil-gas-equipment-services-earnings-estimates-and-revisions-13/))needs resolution. Earnings calls will be essential for determining whether higher crude prices are actually translating into higher profits for the energy complex.

**Trigger 5: Weekly EIA Crude Inventory Reports and SPR Release Announcements.** Supply-side policy responses are the government's primary tool for dampening oil price shocks. A surprise drawdown in commercial inventories would reinforce the bullish price narrative and intensify pressure on credit and equity markets. Conversely, Strategic Petroleum Reserve releases would signal that policymakers view the price spike as severe enough to warrant intervention. In an environment where Nuveen, managing [$463 billion in fixed income assets](https://www.nuveen.com/global/insights/fixed-income), is already flagging oil-driven stress as a primary theme, the policy response channel deserves as much attention as the market price itself.

## Conclusion

The oil-above-100-middle-east-conflict episode unfolding in late March 2026 is not a single-variable story. The evidence from two independent sourcesNuveen's institutional fixed income commentary and Hedge Fund Tips' bottom-up earnings revision dataconverges on a consistent picture: oil prices are rising, Treasury yields are following, credit spreads are widening, and the companies closest to upstream energy activity are seeing their forward earnings marked lower, not higher.

The transmission chain is clear in direction if not yet fully quantified in magnitude. Higher oil feeds into inflation, constraining the Fed. Rising yields and wider spreads tighten financial conditions for every borrower. And the earnings channel is producing a counterintuitive resultOFS estimate revisions running 13 upward to 15 downward with cumulative 2026 earnings power declining 1.80% ([Hedge Fund Tips](https://www.hedgefundtips.com/oil-gas-equipment-services-earnings-estimates-and-revisions-13/))that challenges the simplest version of the energy-winner narrative.

What remains uncertain is substantial. The precise level of oil prices, the magnitude of yield and spread moves, the specific scope of the Middle East conflict, and the root cause of the OFS earnings disconnect are all open questions. The sector-specific margin compression inferred for airlines, chemicals, and trucking is directionally supported but not empirically verified at the individual earnings-estimate level.

The actionable takeaway is that this environment rewards selectivity and punishes complacency. Quality over leverage. Bottom-up credit analysis over geographic or sectoral labels. Sub-sector discrimination within energy rather than blanket exposure. Nuveen's description of a landscape that is "more differentiated, policy-driven, and investable than in recent years" ([Nuveen Fixed Income Insights](https://www.nuveen.com/global/insights/fixed-income)) is not just a marketing taglineit is a survival guide for a market where the obvious trades are not working the way they should.
